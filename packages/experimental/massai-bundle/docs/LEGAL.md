# Legal hygiene: what the provider terms actually require

Not legal advice. A reading of the clauses that bind us, what we changed because of them, and what
only the owner can settle.

Source read: **DeepSeek Open Platform Terms of Service**
(<https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-open-platform-terms-of-service.html>, release
2026-04-22, effective 2026-04-29).

## The good news first

**§1.1 permits exactly what Massai does**: "You can integrate the capabilities of the DeepSeek models
into various downstream systems, applications, or functionalities ... providing services to both
internal and **external end users**." Easy mode is a downstream application, not a breach.

**§4.2 assigns outputs to the developer** ("We assign any rights, title, and interests—if any—in the
Outputs of the Services to you"), so letting users export and keep what they build is clean. The
"take it with you" door has no IP obstacle from the provider's side.

## The four obligations, and how each is now met

| Clause | Obligation | What we shipped |
|---|---|---|
| **§8.1** | "clearly disclose to your end users that the Output content is generated by AI, and may contain errors or omissions" | door fine print: *"Everything Massai writes is generated by AI and may be wrong"* |
| **§3.3** | disclose personal-information processing; honour access / copy / rectification / **erasure** | `/privacy` page + `POST /api/account/erase` (owner-auth, deletes usage, credit and memory rows) |
| **§5.2, §5.3** | no use of "DeepSeek" identifiers in a way that implies partnership, endorsement, or places their mark beside ours | non-affiliation line on the door; provider name kept factual and subordinate; no provider logos anywhere |
| **§7.1(b)** | joint and several liability for end users' breaches | `/aup` page, per-network guest cap, and the one-click freeze in the console |

Also relevant: **§2.2** forbids sharing or exposing the API key, including "in browser or other
client-side code". The key lives only in the server's environment; the guest preview never sends it
to a client, never logs it, never echoes it in an error.

## What remains, and only the owner can do

1. **Business identity, if money changes hands.** Donations ("a coffee") are gifts and are simple.
   Selling credit or a subscription makes Massai the merchant of record: refund policy, GST/HST
   registration past the small-supplier threshold in Canada, and consumer law on prepaid balances.
2. **A real contact address** in `/privacy` and `/terms` — a jurisdiction and an email that someone
   answers. Currently the contact path is the feedback form.
3. **Trademark search on "Massai".** "Maasai" is an ethnonym (a people, not a brand), which is legal
   to use but culturally loaded; a search of CIPO/USPTO/EUIPO before printing merch is cheap insurance.
4. **Regional exposure — MITIGATED.** Google's Gemini API terms require **paid** services when an API
   client is made available to users in the EEA, UK or Switzerland. `packages/experimental/hosted-signin/src/geo.ts`
   now refuses the *free* Gemini route for those countries: the visitor's country is read from the proxy
   header at session mint (`cf-ipcountry` / `x-vercel-ip-country` / `x-geo-country` / `x-country`), remembered
   per session, and the pre-step gate **switches them to DeepSeek** rather than refusing service. Unknown
   country is allowed, because the exposure is identical and refusing the world would break the product.
   Remaining gap: this only works once a proxy supplies the header — direct Render traffic reports no
   country, so nothing is blocked today. Putting Cloudflare in front (a domain, then orange-cloud) closes it.
5. **Export control and sanctions (§9).** Not currently implemented; a geo block is the eventual answer.
6. **Age.** `/terms` and `/privacy` state the service is not directed at children; a jurisdiction-aware
   age gate would harden it.
7. **Model output in regulated domains.** Providers disclaim medical/legal/financial advice; `/terms`
   §2 says the same. If someone uses Massai to build something regulated, that is their compliance
   problem — worth saying plainly in the AUP if that traffic ever appears.

## The one-line summary

The clauses that could have killed easy mode do not: reselling *a service* built on the API is
expressly allowed, and the outputs are ours to hand to users. What the terms demand is disclosure —
of AI, of privacy handling, of non-affiliation — and that is now on every door in.
