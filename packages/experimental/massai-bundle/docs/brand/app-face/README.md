# The app face — the mask that sits beside the name

The Flower of Life is the logo (the door, the tab, the tile). This face is the *companion*: it goes
directly beside the wordmark **inside** the app, on the pages that belong to the workshop rather than
the shopfront.

## Why this face

The reference set divided cleanly by gender read, and that is worth naming because it decided the
choice:

| Cue | Reads |
|---|---|
| tall **curved** horns, filigree, flower worn as a jewel or crown | feminine, regal |
| **straight facets**, slit eyes, hard jaw, no ornament | masculine, martial |
| flower at the chest | neutral / madonna |
| flower at the crown | regal, effeminate |

The owner's own favourite was the plain **faceted** one — no flower, no filigree. It is also the only
one that survives being 32px tall next to a wordmark, so taste and engineering agree.

## The three studies

| File | What it is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `a-faceted.svg` → `face-512.png` | six facets, dark visor wedge, two ember slit eyes | **chosen** |
| `b-hybrid.svg` → `face-flower-512.png` | the same helm with the Flower of Life as a brow stone | ties the two marks together; the flower turns to mush below ~48px, so use it only large |
| `c-triple.svg` | three facet spearheads, eyes cut out | reads as a gem or an insect, not a face |

`with-wordmark.png` is the honest test: each face at 34px beside the serif wordmark.

## The rule for using it

- **≥48px** — either face; the hybrid shows its flower and earns the connection to the logo.
- **24–48px** — `a-faceted` only.
- **≤24px** — drop it entirely and show the wordmark alone. A face at 16px is a gold smudge.

## What it means

Six planes, two lit slits, one dark wedge. No mouth: the face is *watching*, not speaking. Gold on
black, the same #D9B463 ramp as the flower, ember #FF8A00 only inside the eyes.
